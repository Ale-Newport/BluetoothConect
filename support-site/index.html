What AirLink is

AirLink connects two phones directly to each other over Bluetooth and, when both are on the same Wi-Fi network, over that network as well. There is no server in the middle and no account to create. It works in airplane mode, on a plane, in a tunnel, in a field — anywhere the two phones are close enough to hear each other.

Once two phones are connected you can:

  • Chat — text, emoji, replies, reactions, photos and voice messages.
  • Play — around 28 two-player games, from chess to air hockey.
  • Share — send a file of any kind, with a real progress bar that survives a reconnect.
  • Watch together — play the same video on both phones, in step.

AirLink needs two devices. With one phone there is nothing to see. The home screen will honestly say that nobody is nearby, because nobody is. That is the app working correctly, not a fault. Everything in AirLink happens between two phones that have found each other.

Connecting two phones for the first time

  1. Open AirLink on both phones and keep both apps on screen while you connect.
  2. Allow Bluetooth when iOS asks. If both phones are on the same Wi-Fi, allow Local Network too — that is what makes photos and files fast.
  3. Wait a few seconds. Each phone lists the other under Other devices.
  4. On one phone, tap Connect next to the other phone's name.
  5. Both phones now show the same six digits. Compare them out loud, and confirm on both phones only if they match.
  6. That is it. You are now friends, and the two phones recognise each other automatically from then on — no code, no confirmation, and nothing to sign in to.

You can also skip the six digits by scanning a QR code: on one phone go to You → Show my QR code, and on the other You → Scan a friend.

Why the six digits matter

The digits are calculated from the encrypted handshake itself. If somebody were sitting between the two phones relaying the conversation, the two phones would necessarily compute different codes. Matching digits are proof you are talking to the phone you think you are. If the codes differ, cancel and try again somewhere else.

Troubleshooting

1. AirLink says Bluetooth permission is off

This is a permission you declined, not a switch you forgot. Open the iPhone Settings app → scroll to AirLink → turn Bluetooth on. (The banner inside AirLink distinguishes this from Bluetooth actually being switched off in Control Centre, and from a device with no Bluetooth radio at all, so read which of the three it says.)

Without Bluetooth, AirLink can still find friends on the same Wi-Fi network — but it cannot work with no network at all, which is the whole point of the app.

2. The two phones are not on the same Wi-Fi

Wi-Fi is optional but it is far faster, so it is worth getting right. Both phones must be on the same network — not one on Wi-Fi and one on mobile data, and not two different networks in the same building. Guest networks and some hotel and office networks block devices from seeing each other entirely; if that is what you are on, turn Wi-Fi off on both phones and let AirLink use Bluetooth.

Both phones must also have allowed Local Network access for AirLink (Settings → AirLink → Local Network). If you declined it once, iOS never asks again — you have to turn it on there yourself.

3. One phone is on an older version of AirLink

Two phones running versions that do not speak the same protocol will simply not list each other. AirLink hides a device it cannot talk to rather than offering you a connection that would fail halfway through, so an old build looks exactly like no device at all.

Update AirLink from the App Store on both phones, force-quit the app on both (swipe up from the bottom and flick the card away), and reopen it.

4. The two phones still cannot see each other

Work through these in order — the first two fix most cases:

  • Keep AirLink on screen on both phones. iOS heavily restricts what an app in the background may broadcast, and a locked phone is much harder for the other one to find.
  • Get closer. Bluetooth is a few metres through open air and much less through a bag, a pocket, a wall or a body.
  • Switch Bluetooth off and on again in Settings on both phones. The iOS Bluetooth stack occasionally gets stuck; this clears it.
  • Force-quit and reopen AirLink on both phones.
  • Check you have not blocked each other. A blocked device is never shown. Look under You → Friends and tap Unblock if the name is there.
  • Restart both phones. Rarely needed, but it is the last thing that fixes a wedged radio.

Airplane mode is fine, as long as Bluetooth is switched back on afterwards — iOS remembers Bluetooth separately once you turn it on inside airplane mode.

Photos, voice messages and notifications

Photos are picked from your photo library, and voice messages are recorded with the microphone control in the chat. iOS asks for Photos and Microphone access the first time you use each, and AirLink asks for each only at that moment — not on first launch.

Both travel over the same direct link as your text messages. Over Bluetooth alone a photo can take minutes; AirLink shows an honest estimate rather than a spinner. If you can get both phones onto the same Wi-Fi, it takes seconds instead.

If you allow Notifications, AirLink can tell you about a new message or a game invitation while you are in another part of the app or another app entirely. These are created by your own phone from something the other phone just sent you over the direct link — there is no push service and no server involved, so nothing about your messages passes through anyone else's computer. Unread items are also marked with a badge on the tab bar inside the app, whether or not you allow notifications.

Blocking and reporting someone

Press and hold any message from the other person → Report and block. That blocks the person, drops the connection immediately, and deletes the conversation from your phone. They cannot connect to you or be connected to again unless you unblock them under You → Friends.

You can also block a device directly from You → Friends → Block device.

We want to be straight with you about what reporting can and cannot do here. Messages in AirLink travel directly between two phones and never reach us — there is no server, so we never had a copy of anything and there is nothing for us to review, take down or pass to anyone. What blocking does is the part that actually protects you: it ends the connection and prevents another one. If you want to tell us what happened anyway, please write to the address below.

Deleting your data

Everything AirLink knows lives on your phone: your messages, the files you have sent and received, your friends list and your keys. You → Settings → Clear all history erases conversations, and deleting the app removes all of it. There is no account to close, because there was never an account.
